01 / Follow the physical chain

The ocean is part
of the instrument.

A recorded spectrum is the result of several mechanisms, not a label attached to an animal or a machine.

  1. s(t)

    Source

    A pressure history with a chosen amplitude, bandwidth and time structure. Here, the source library is synthetic.

  2. h(t)

    Channel

    Spreading changes amplitude; travel time changes arrival; a surface-reflected copy changes interference.

  3. y(t)

    Receiver

    An assumed response and noise alter what remains measurable. A generic model is not a calibrated hydrophone.

A first pass through the lab

  1. Listen: hold the source fixed and change only range. Then add the surface.
  2. Read: distinguish waveform, spectrum and spectrogram before comparing levels.
  3. Locate: correlate a known pulse with its return, then inspect the inferred range.
  4. Question: separate a verified calculation from a validated ocean model.

02 / Passive acoustics

Listen without
transmitting.

The question is not just “what made this sound?” It is “what did the journey leave behind?”

Change one thing.

Start with the whistle-like source and the direct path. Compare the available ranges without changing the source. Inspect received pressure before looking at the spectrum.

Now include the surface reflection at the same range. A spectral notch need not mean that the source stopped producing that frequency: the paths can cancel at the receiver.

Look forArrival time in the waveform. Relative level in the PSD. Frequency-dependent interference when the reflected path is present.

What is in the source library?
Whistle-like
A tonal, time-varying signature for following frequency through time. It is not a species model.
Click-like
Brief pulses spread energy across a band. A waveform resolves timing; a spectrogram shows repeated events.
Vessel-like
A phenomenological mixture of tonal and broadband content. No machinery identification or validated cavitation model is implied.

Source level, duration, seed and spectral construction are model parameters, not inferred field measurements. Exact executed values belong to each case’s downloaded metadata.

Amplitude convention: taper the finite emission record, then scale its RMS at the nominal 1 m reference. Quiet intervals count in a click train. Equal source RMS does not mean equal peak pressure, equal audibility or equal received SNR.

Sampling / Hz
Sample rate sets the Nyquist limit; segment length and window set spectral-estimator resolution.
Duration / s
The unpadded emission duration is distinct from the longer propagated and filtered record.
Seed / integer
Controls the declared stochastic construction. Repeatability is not evidence of a realistic population of sources.

Geometry & model limits

Range is horizontal separation, not slant distance. The source and receiver are stationary, at 8 m and 12 m depth. Depth is positive downward. The homogeneous-water sound speed is 1500 m/s.

Pressure scales as 1/r in an idealized three-dimensional free-space model, with source amplitude referenced to 1 m. The optional ideal surface has reflection coefficient −1.

No seabed, refraction, absorption, source motion or Doppler is included. Real water can violate every one of those simplifications.

The generic passive receiver uses a causal lowpass FIR. Its receiver group delay is retained, so a filtered arrival is not just the geometric travel time. Noise is filtered through the same assumed response. The source RMS window and the receiver analysis window are distinct.

Go deeper / The two-path transfer function

Distance becomes delay and phase.

Let R denote horizontal separation, zs source depth, zr receiver depth and c sound speed. The direct path and the path to the image source have different lengths.

The path difference sets the relative arrival time Δτ. In frequency space, that delay becomes phase. With the ideal negative surface reflection, the two complex contributions subtract; cancellation depends on frequency and geometry.

This expression describes the ideal propagation channel, before receiver filtering and noise. Numerical fractional-delay accuracy is a separate verification question.

rd=R2+(zr−zs)2,rs=R2+(zr+zs)2
Δτ=rs−rdc
H(f)=r0rde−i2πfτd−r0rse−i2πfτs

r0 = 1 m; τd and τs are path delays on the declared source clock. Removing the surface means omitting the second term, not changing the source.

03 / Reading the instruments

A decibel needs
a reference.

Pressure level, spectral density and listening loudness answer different questions.

Pressure level

How much pressure?

RMS pressure summarizes a specified time window. Underwater sound pressure level uses a reference of 1 µPa, not the airborne reference.

Lp=20log10(prms1µPa)

Reported as dB re 1 µPa. The RMS window matters, especially for intermittent clicks.

Power spectral density

How is that pressure distributed?

PSD is mean-square pressure per unit frequency, in Pa²/Hz. A narrow spectral peak is not the same quantity as a band-integrated pressure level.

LS(f)=10log10(Spp(f)10−12Pa2/Hz)

Reported as dB re 1 µPa²/Hz. Average or integrate linear PSD first; do not average decibels to obtain a band level.

Time–frequency structure

When is each frequency present?

A spectrogram applies a spectral estimator to successive windows. A longer window resolves nearby frequencies more finely but blurs timing. Overlap provides more closely spaced estimates, not independent new observations.

Welch averaging reduces the variability of a PSD estimate. Window shape, segment length, overlap, detrending and one-sided normalization all affect interpretation; inspect the saved analysis settings before comparing panels.

Three normalizations. Three meanings.

Source normalization sets a synthetic amplitude. PSD normalization gives a density whose linear band sum estimates mean-square pressure. Listening normalization adjusts digital playback level. A normalized audio clip is not evidence of an absolute underwater level.

04 / Active acoustics

Know the pulse.
Find the return.

Passive listening asks what is present. Active sensing adds a known transmitted waveform and asks how it came back.

From echo to range.

This experiment is a stationary, analytic point target. The signal travels out and back; a scalar scattering factor represents the return. It is not an animal, a distributed body or a validated target-strength model.

A matched filter slides a copy of the known pulse across the noisy received record. The correlation peak is a candidate delay. Converting that two-way delay to range is the next, separate step.

Try thisKeep target range fixed and change input SNR. Compare the raw trace with the correlation output. Then change range and check whether the peak moves to the expected delay.

What does the SNR control mean?

The benchmark defines input SNR using clean-echo mean-square pressure on the echo support, divided by the ensemble white-noise variance. It is a per-sample, full-Nyquist-band ratio, not a chirp-band or output SNR. A finite noise realization need not equal that requested ratio exactly.

Noise variance is recomputed for each target range and requested SNR. A fixed-SNR range comparison is not a range-performance curve at fixed ambient noise. The exact noise level, support and seed are in each case’s metadata.

SNR is not a universal property of a recording: measuring noise over the whole record and measuring the echo over only its pulse window can give different ratios. Do not compare values without matching definitions.

What a correlation peak cannot prove

A peak is not a calibrated detection probability. False-alarm rates need independent noise-only trials and a specified search region and threshold. Localization error and an any-lag alarm are different outcomes.

The signed correlation is divided by the square root of template energy and the known simulation noise standard deviation. Its dimensionless score is not a pressure, a PSD or a probability. The range estimator selects the largest absolute score and always returns a range, even if noise wins.

Separate timing from detection.

Read the transmitted pulse, noisy return and matched-filter lag together. Compare the known target range with the estimate. Agreement in a controlled synthetic case checks the delay-to-range calculation; it does not establish performance in reverberation, multipath, motion or an unknown environment.

Go deeper / Correlation, lag and two-way range

The factor of two is physical.

For real sampled signals, correlate the received record x with the template s. Let k̂ be the selected lag in samples and fs the sample rate. A monostatic point-target delay includes both the outward and return journeys.

Ordinary matched correlation has familiar optimality properties under a known template and additive white-noise assumptions. Coloured noise, reverberation and template mismatch require more care.

The sample spacing of the lag axis is not the same as the ability to separate two targets. Pulse bandwidth, waveform ambiguity and noise all matter. Neither a sharp peak nor a fine grid removes model uncertainty.

C[k]=∑nx[n]s[n−k]
τ^=k^fs,R^=cτ^2

Conceptual real-signal correlation. The producer declares any output normalization and peak-selection convention in the exported metadata.

05 / An observational anchor

A recording is
a different kind
of evidence.

Use a documented reference to inspect structure, not to retroactively calibrate a synthetic source.

Provenance travels with the data.

A useful reference identifies the recording source, reuse conditions, processing and calibration status. Similar-looking spectrograms alone do not validate a biological source model or a receiver.

From a recording to a spectrum

The offline recording pipeline preserves the original file, selects a declared channel, checks sample counts and finite values, flags near-full-scale samples, and retains a content hash. Potential saturation is a diagnostic, not proof of hardware clipping.

Without calibration, waveform and PSD remain in digital amplitude and digital amplitude²/Hz. A pressure conversion needs known ADC scaling, gain and sensitivity. A declared flat scalar calibration is not a frequency-dependent calibration certificate.

Analysis exports keep their units and estimator settings. A decoded WAV does not by itself establish deployment timing, sensor orientation, source distance or label truth.

07 / Methods & trust

A working model
is not a measured
ocean.

Verification asks whether the calculation implements the intended equations. Validation asks whether those equations adequately describe the real phenomenon.

Verify the implementation.

Check units, deterministic seeds, propagation delay, spectral normalization and point-target range recovery against independent expectations. These checks can expose numerical mistakes.

Validate the physical claim.

Compare with independent, suitably calibrated observations and account for measurement and model uncertainty. An ideal surface, synthetic source or assumed receiver does not become field-validated because software tests pass.
